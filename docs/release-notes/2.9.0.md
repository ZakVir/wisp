## Wisp 2.9.0

**Computer use, step by step.** When you ask Wisp to open an app or search a site, it now works like a person at
the keyboard, looking at the screen before each step. The cursor animation and the real click and typing happen
together:

- **It looks first.** Before each click Wisp reads where things are right now (through Accessibility): the Dock's
  icons, the Apps launcher's search field and results, the browser's address bar, the site's search box.
- **Hidden Dock?** If a full-screen app is covering the Dock (or the Dock auto-hides), Wisp brings it up by moving
  the pointer to the screen edge, waits until it has slid in, then clicks. The pointer goes back where it was.
- **Not in the Dock?** The cursor clicks **Apps** in the Dock, clicks the search field, types the app's name, then
  clicks the app in the results, and it opens as the cursor clicks.
- **Checked as it goes.** After typing, Wisp reads the field back and retypes if it doesn't match; if the launcher
  closes on its own, Wisp opens it again and carries on; it waits until the app is really in front.
- **Deliberate pace.** The cursor moves to each target at an easy, human pace so you can follow every step.
- **No pretending.** Without the Accessibility permission (Better answers), Wisp opens the app or page directly,
  shows macOS's permission prompt once, and tells you how to turn it on, instead of miming clicks it can't do.

Requires macOS 15 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon.
